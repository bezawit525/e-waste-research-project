# Awareness, Disposal Practices, and Readiness for Digital E-Waste Solutions among University Students in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia

## 1. Project Overview

This research project examines university students' awareness of electronic waste (e-waste), their current disposal practices, and their readiness to adopt digital solutions for e-waste management in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia.

The study focuses on understanding how students handle obsolete electronic devices and identifying the factors that influence their willingness to use digital platforms, such as mobile applications or web-based systems, for e-waste disposal.

The research follows an instructor-approved proposal and adopts a quantitative, cross-sectional survey design.

## 2. Research Questions

1. What is the level of awareness and what are the current disposal practices of university students in Addis Ababa regarding e-waste?
2. What factors influence students' willingness to use digital solutions (e.g., a mobile app or web platform) for e-waste disposal?

## 3. Research Objectives

### General Objective

To assess university students' e-waste awareness and disposal practices in Addis Ababa and evaluate their readiness to adopt digital solutions for e-waste management.

### Specific Objectives

1. To measure students' awareness of e-waste hazards and identify how they currently dispose of obsolete electronics.
2. To identify the factors (perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and environmental awareness) that influence their willingness to use a digital e-waste solution.

## 4. Research Methodology

The study uses a quantitative research approach with a cross-sectional survey design.

| Component                  | Description                                                        |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Research approach          | Quantitative                                                       |
| Research design            | Cross-sectional survey                                             |
| Target population          | Undergraduate university students in Addis Ababa                   |
| Sampling technique         | Convenience sampling                                               |
| Target sample size         | Approximately 100 respondents                                      |
| Data source                | Primary data                                                       |
| Data collection instrument | Structured questionnaire                                           |
| Data collection methods    | Google Forms and paper questionnaires                              |
| Data analysis              | Descriptive statistics, correlation, and linear regression         |
| Analysis software          | SPSS or Microsoft Excel                                            |
| Ethical considerations     | Voluntary participation, informed consent, and anonymous responses |

The questionnaire consists of three main sections:

- **Part A:** Demographic information.
- **Part B:** E-waste awareness and disposal practices.
- **Part C:** Likert-scale questions adapted from the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), covering perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and behavioral intention to use a digital e-waste platform.

## 6. Data Collection and Analysis

Primary data will be collected from approximately 100 undergraduate university students in Addis Ababa using structured questionnaires.

The collected data will be analyzed according to the research questions.

### Research Question 1

Descriptive statistical techniques will be used to examine students' awareness and disposal practices, including:

- Frequencies
- Percentages
- Means, where appropriate

### Research Question 2

Inferential statistical techniques will be used to examine factors associated with students' willingness to adopt digital e-waste management solutions, including:

- Correlation analysis
- Linear regression analysis

The analysis will focus on perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and environmental awareness.
